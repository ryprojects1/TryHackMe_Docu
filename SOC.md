# SOC Level 1

## What is an Alert?

An alert is a notification generated by a security solution, checked manually by SOC analysts. Without alerts, it would be impossible to manually check the millions of events that occur daily.

| Solution | Examples | Description |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| SIEM System | Splunk ES, Elastic | Solid alert management, perfect choice for most SOC teams |
| EDR or NDR | MS Defender, CrowdStrike | Provide their own dashboards but preferred to route through SIEM or SOAR |
| SOAR System | Splunk SOAR, Cortex SOAR | Used by bigger SOC teams to aggregate and centralise alerts |
| ITSM System | Jira, TheHive | Some teams use a dedicated ticket management solution |

---

## SOC Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| L1 Analyst | Reviews alerts, distinguishes real threats from noise, notifies L2 of real threats |
| L2 Analyst | Receives escalated alerts from L1, performs deeper analysis and remediation |
| SOC Engineer | Ensures alerts contain enough information for efficient triage |
| SOC Manager | Tracks speed and quality of alert triage |

---

## Alert Verdict

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| 🔴 True Positive | Real threat |
| 🟢 False Positive | No threat / noise |

---

## Picking the Right Alert

**Step 1 - Filter**
Only take new, unseen, unresolved alerts. Never take one already being handled by a teammate.

**Step 2 - Sort by severity**
Critical > High > Medium > Low. Critical alerts are far more likely to be real, high-impact threats.

**Step 3 - Sort by time**
Oldest first. If two active breaches exist, the older one has had more time to cause damage.

---

## Initial Actions

1. Assign the alert to yourself
2. Move it to In Progress
3. Familiarise yourself with the alert name, description, and key indicators

---

## Investigation

If no workbook or playbook is available, use these as your guide:

- **Who** is affected: user, hostname, cloud, network, or website
- **What** happened: suspicious login, malware, phishing
- **When**: review events shortly before and after the alert
- **Why**: use threat intelligence platforms to verify your findings

---

## Final Actions

1. Decide if it is a True Positive or False Positive
2. Write a detailed comment explaining your steps and verdict
3. Move the alert to Closed
4. Escalate to L2 if the alert requires deeper investigation

---

## Alert Reporting

| Purpose | Why it matters |
|---------|---------------|
| Context for escalation | Saves L2 time, helps them understand what happened without starting from scratch |
| Record keeping | SIEM logs last 3-12 months but alerts are kept indefinitely |
| Skill development | Writing reports forces you to fully understand what you investigated |

---

## Report Format - The 5 Ws

| W | What to include |
|---|----------------|
| Who | User, account, or entity involved |
| What | The exact action or event sequence |
| When | Start and end time of the suspicious activity |
| Where | Device, IP address, or website involved |
| Why | Your reasoning behind the final verdict |
